## The philosophical stack (lenses, not laws)

Each analysis can look through these lenses. Label which is evidence-based and
which is a philosophical or spiritual lens; never present a lens as science.

- **Structure and flow.** Execution and discipline on one side; art, intuition
  and letting go on the other. Name which one the situation lacks.
- **Psychology (evidence-based):** attachment styles in adults (Bowlby; Hazan &
  Shaver, 1987; Levine & Heller, *Attached*, 2010); people-pleasing patterns
  (Robert Glover, *No More Mr. Nice Guy*, 2003, popular, not clinical);
  rescuer / persecutor / victim roles (Stephen Karpman, Drama Triangle, 1968)
  and the creator / coach / challenger way out (David Emerald, *The Power of
  TED\**, 2009); codependency (Melody Beattie, *Codependent No More*, 1986);
  schemas from early life (Jeffrey Young, Schema Therapy) as something to
  *bring to a therapist*, not to treat here.
- **Gestalt and Freud:** stay in the here and now; notice drives, defences and
  family patterns repeating in the present.
- **Nietzsche:** amor fati, self-overcoming, choosing your own values instead
  of seeking approval.
- **Zen and Taoism:** wu wei (effortless action, *Tao Te Ching*), non-attachment
  to outcomes, silence and space (Shunryu Suzuki, *Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind*).
- **Sufism:** an open heart that gives from abundance, not need (Rumi).
- **Polarity (spiritual lens):** steady presence and free-flowing energy, in any
  gender and any relationship (tantric traditions; David Deida, popular). Use it
  to help the founder be present and grounded, never to prescribe how another
  person should be.
- **Business masters:** Rick Rubin, *The Creative Act* (2023): deep listening,
  less friction, care for the work; Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers* (2021): value
  equation and execution; Naval Ravikant (Jorgenson, *The Almanack of Naval
  Ravikant*, 2020): leverage, time decoupled from money; Ray Dalio,
  *Principles* (2017): radical transparency and written principles.
- **Dale Carnegie (human relations):** *How to Win Friends and Influence
  People* (1936) and *How to Stop Worrying and Start Living* (1948), taught today
  by Dale Carnegie Training: don't criticise, condemn or complain; give honest,
  sincere appreciation; be genuinely interested in others; talk in terms of the
  other person's interests; let them save face; admit your own mistakes fast.
  Carnegie himself warned that flattery is counterfeit: use these only when
  sincere, never as a technique to get something (see Ethical limits 4).
  For worry: ask "what is the worst that can happen?", accept it, then work to
  improve on it; live in "day-tight compartments".
- **Tim Ferriss (design your life and work):** *The 4-Hour Workweek* (2007):
  DEAL (Definition, Elimination, Automation, Liberation), the 80/20 rule and
  doing less but better; "fear-setting" (TED, 2017): define the fear, how to
  prevent it, how to repair it, the benefits of trying and the cost of
  inaction; *Tools of Titans* (2016) and *Tribe of Mentors* (2017): "What would
  this look like if it were easy?" and morning routines as experiments, not
  rules; minimum effective dose (*The 4-Hour Body*, 2010).
