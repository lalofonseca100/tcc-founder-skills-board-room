# Sources for founder-legal

Jurisdictions: international, EU, Netherlands, Spain, Mexico, United States (Texas first; California and New York kept for gigs and clients there).

Every legal statement in a report cites one of these ids, with its status.

**Status** (same words as founder-tax and founder-learning):

- `law`: the official text (or official status table) was read on the date shown.
- `law-index`: the law exists and is in force, but the article wording has not been read
  verbatim yet. Fine to name the law; read the article before quoting it or relying on a detail.
- `proposal`: announced or passed but not yet in force (or stalled).
- `unverified`: only a secondary source (law firm, news, blog) so far.
- `guideline`: soft law, model contracts, codes of conduct. Useful for benchmarking, not binding.

Rule: a report may rely on a detail only when its source is `law`. Anything else is said
with its status ("según X, todavía sin confirmar en el texto oficial").

## Open checks (close in the first review)

1. Read verbatim: Waadi arts. 1 and 3 and the 2004 change on artists (Kst 28465); decide with a lawyer whether an artist commission or a one-time artist fee is allowed when the artist works as a freelancer.
2. Read the Kamerbrief of 19 Dec 2025 (DBA enforcement 2026) and watch the Staatsblad for the VBAR entry-into-force date and the Zelfstandigenwet.
3. Confirm US status of HCCH-2005 on the status table.
4. Read LFDA arts. 18-33 and LFPDPPP 2025 consent articles before quoting them.
5. Mexico LFT art. 12-15 text and the RACT current text on DOF.
6. Platform Work Directive: Dutch transposition bill and whether a booking platform falls under it.
7. Spain: read RD 607/2026 (BOE-A-2026-16173) before 25 May 2027 and update clauses C-20 and the management checklist; read LPI arts. 14 and 43-57 and Ley 3/2004 art. 4.
8. Texas: read 15.50-15.52 for 2025 changes; read Dresser v. Page; confirm no talent-agency licence was reintroduced after 2011.
