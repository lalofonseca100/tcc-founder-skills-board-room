# Clause library (founder-legal)

Each clause has: **why** it exists, a **win-win default** (what we offer first), our
**fallback** (the least we accept), **red lines** (walk away or lawyer), and
**jurisdiction notes** with source ids from `sources.md`. Draft in the counterpart's
language in **English or Spanish** (the two languages we are ready for; a Dutch
counterpart gets English unless Dutch is required); name one controlling language.

Win-win test for every clause (from GTY): (1) does it protect a real interest of
each side, (2) is it mirrored unless there is a reason not to, (3) is there an
objective standard behind it (law, sector code, market practice), (4) would we sign
it if the roles were reversed?

---

## C-01 Parties and roles
- **Why**: who is bound, in which capacity (eenmanszaak, B.V., Stichting, persona
  física con RFC, S.A.P.I., LLC). The wrong party means no claim.
- **Default**: full legal name, registration number (KvK, RFC, EIN), address, signatory
  and their authority. Say who is agent and who is principal when we book on behalf of
  someone (agency vs. own account changes VAT, liability and placement rules).
- **Red line**: signing personally for a company that does not exist yet.

## C-02 Scope and deliverables
- **Default**: a numbered list, what is *not* included, assumptions, client inputs and
  their dates. Changes only by a signed change order with price and time.
- **Fallback**: hourly rate for out-of-scope work agreed in advance.
- **Notes**: NL services are an *opdracht* (BW7-400): duty of care, not a result,
  unless a result is promised. MX: *prestación de servicios profesionales* (CCF).

## C-03 Price, deposit and payment
- **Default**: price excl. VAT; deposit 30-50% on signing (non-refundable once costs are
  incurred); balance 14 days after delivery or the event; currency named; who pays bank
  and FX costs.
- **Fallback**: 30 days; milestones for long jobs.
- **Red line**: more than 60 days B2B without a reason (LATE-PAY-DIR).
- **Notes**: statutory commercial interest NL (BW6-119A) and EUR 40 minimum costs;
  MX: agree interest in writing (legal rate in Código de Comercio is low); ES: 30 days
  default, max 60 B2B, statutory interest (ES-MOROSIDAD); US: state usury caps apply
  to high interest (Texas included).

## C-04 Late payment
- **Default**: after a written reminder with 14 days: statutory interest + collection
  costs; right to pause work after a further 7 days.
- **Win-win**: a one-call "payment problem" conversation before costs start.

## C-05 Acceptance
- **Default**: client reviews within 5 working days; silence = accepted; only material
  defects block acceptance; two rounds of corrections included.

## C-06 Term, termination and exit
- **Default**: fixed term or until delivery; either side may end for material breach not
  cured in 14 days; for convenience with notice and payment for work done + committed costs.
- **Notes**: NL 7:408 lets a client end an *opdracht* at any time unless excluded (B2B
  can exclude it, BW7-400). Automatic renewal: diarise; B2C limits apply (BW6-AV).
- **Exit duties**: hand-over of files, data return or deletion (GDPR art. 28), final invoice.

## C-07 Cancellation and postponement (events, gigs, installations)
- **Default** sliding scale, mirrored for both sides:
  more than 90 days before: 25% of fee; 30-90 days: 50%; under 30 days: 100%
  (plus non-recoverable costs already spent). Postponement within 12 months keeps the
  deposit as credit once.
- **Win-win**: weather and permit risk named and shared; if the client loses the permit,
  the artist still gets the costs.

## C-08 Force majeure
- **Default**: events outside control (weather alerts by authority, government bans,
  epidemics, strikes not of the party); notice within 48 h; deposit kept against costs;
  either side may end after 30 days.
- **Notes**: NL *overmacht* BW 6:75; MX *caso fortuito/fuerza mayor* CCF art. 2111;
  US: only what the contract says (no general doctrine), so list events.

## C-09 Limitation of liability
- **Default (mutual)**: cap at the fees of the contract (or 12 months of fees); no
  indirect or consequential loss; cap does not apply to intent/gross negligence, death or
  injury, confidentiality and data breaches caused by the party, or unpaid fees.
- **Fallback**: cap at 2x fees or at the insured amount, whichever is higher.
- **Red line**: unlimited liability for the smaller party; liability for the client's
  own staff, venue or public.
- **Notes**: NL: exclusion for intent/gross negligence of the company itself is void
  (BW 6:248 lid 2 case law); grey list for consumers. MX: no waiver for *dolo* (CCF art. 2106).

## C-10 Indemnity
- **Default (mutual)**: each side covers third-party claims caused by its own breach,
  IP infringement or negligence, with prompt notice and control of the defence.
- **Texas**: an indemnity or release that covers the other party's *own negligence* must
  say so in express words and be conspicuous (bold, capitals, heading), or it fails
  (TX-FAIRNOTICE). Draft it that way only when we want it; refuse it when it is against us.
- **Red line**: "any and all claims" regardless of fault.

## C-11 Insurance
- **Default**: each side keeps liability insurance (amount named); event/art jobs: who
  insures the work in transit, on site and the public liability (*AVB* in NL).

## C-12 Intellectual property
- **Default**: each keeps what it had before (tools, templates, know-how, artworks).
  Client gets a licence or ownership of the *specific deliverables* **after full payment**,
  for the agreed use, media, territory and time. Portfolio use allowed unless confidential.
- **Artists**: licence, not transfer, of artworks and images; credit always.
- **Software/AI**: client owns custom code on payment; we keep reusable components and
  give a perpetual licence to them inside the client's product; open-source licences listed.
- **Notes**: NL transfer needs a signed deed (AW art. 2); makers' rights (AW 25b-25h,
  DSM-COPY): fair pay, report on use, end the deal for non-use. MX: transfer in writing,
  paid and temporary (LFDA). US: work-for-hire only for listed categories; otherwise a
  signed assignment (USC17 204(a)). ES: transfers in writing; if time and territory are
  missing, the law limits them (ES-LPI art. 43); moral rights cannot be waived (art. 14).

## C-13 Moral rights and credit
- **Default**: credit line agreed; no changes that harm the work's integrity without consent.
- **Notes**: cannot be waived in MX (LFDA) or ES (ES-LPI); limited waiver in NL (AW art. 25).

## C-14 Non-solicitation / introduction fee (instead of exclusivity)
- **Default**: if the client books an artist, supplier or person we introduced directly
  within 12 months, it pays us 15% of the fees (or a fixed fee). No ban, just a fair fee.
- **Why win-win**: the artist stays free; we are paid for the introduction.
- **Notes**: NL relationship clauses for freelancers are judged on reasonableness; a
  fee is easier to enforce than a ban.

## C-15 Exclusivity
- **Default**: avoid. If asked: limited by territory, type of work and time (max 12
  months), and tied to a minimum performance (e.g. minimum bookings or income);
  otherwise it ends.

## C-16 Non-compete
- **Default**: avoid with freelancers; with co-founders, narrow (same business, 12
  months, region) and paid.
- **Notes**: void in California (CA-BP-16600); Texas enforces reasonable ones
  ancillary to another agreement (TX-NONCOMPETE); limited in MX by CPEUM-5; NL employees
  only in writing and with reasons for fixed-term contracts (BW 7:653).

## C-17 Confidentiality
- **Default (mutual)**: 2-3 years after the end (trade secrets as long as secret); usual
  exceptions (public, known before, required by law); return or delete on request.

## C-18 Personal data
- **Default**: if we process personal data for a client: a processor agreement
  (GDPR art. 28) with security measures, sub-processors list, breach notice in 48 h,
  deletion at the end. Transfers outside the EEA: SCC-2021. Mexico: aviso de privacidad,
  express written consent for sensitive data (LFPDPPP-2025).

## C-19 AI use (agency and software work)
- **Default**: we say which AI tools we use; client data is not used to train third-party
  models (choose vendor settings that guarantee this); human review of outputs; client
  confirms it may use the data it gives us; for AI systems we build, a short note on
  roles under the AI Act (who is provider, who is deployer) and transparency duties;
  Texas consumers: check TX-TRAIGA disclosure and biometric rules.

## C-20 Independent status (freelancers, artists, drivers)
- **Default**: each party works as an independent business, sets its own hours and
  methods, may work for others, uses own tools, invoices with VAT where due, bears its own risk.
- **Notes**: labels do not decide; the facts do. NL: DBA-2025 enforcement and the
  coming presumption below EUR 38/h (VBAR-2026); Belastingdienst model agreements
  (BD-MODEL). MX: personnel subcontracting banned (LFT-2021). ES: an artist performing for
  a promoter can be an employee under the special regime (ES-RD1435 until 24 May 2027,
  then ES-RD607-2026); a freelancer with 75%+ of income from us is a TRADE (ES-LETA).
  US: ABC test in California (CA-AB5); Texas uses the common-law control test.

## C-21 Taxes and VAT
- Who invoices whom, VAT reverse charge for EU B2B, artist withholding where they
  perform (treaty art. 17). Always hand the facts to **founder-tax**; the contract says
  "prices exclude VAT; each party pays its own taxes; withholding per law with certificate".

## C-22 Assignment and subcontracting
- **Default**: no transfer of the contract without consent (not unreasonably refused);
  subcontracting allowed with the main party staying responsible.

## C-23 Governing law and disputes
- **Chosen case by case** (founder's rule, 2026-11-02): no fixed home court. The ladder is
  always (1) talk between decision makers within 14 days, (2) mediation, (3) a forum
  picked for *this* deal with the guide below, seat and language named.
- **Forum guide**: where are the counterpart's assets (a judgment must be enforced there)?
  Is the amount worth arbitration costs (rule of thumb: under EUR 25k prefer a court or
  small-claims route)? Do both sides trust a neutral seat? Options: courts of the
  counterpart's or our seat; arbitration NAI (NL), ICC (international), CAM or CANACO
  (Mexico), Corte de Arbitraje de Madrid or CIMA (Spain), AAA-ICDR (US/Texas).
- **Why arbitration across borders**: awards are enforced in 170+ countries (NYC-1958);
  EU court judgments travel inside the EU (BRUSSELS-IA) and to Mexico via HCCH-2005.
- **Notes**: Rome I lets parties choose the law (ROME-I art. 3); consumers keep their own
  protections. NY law/courts need the GOL thresholds (NY-GOL-1401); Texas has its own
  rule for large deals (TX-CHOICE) and a state arbitration act (TX-ARB); Spain: Ley 60/2003
  (ES-ARB). CISG applies to
  international goods sales unless excluded: say "CISG applies" or "CISG is excluded", on purpose.

## C-24 Notices, changes, signatures
- Changes only in writing signed by both (e-signature fine: ESIGN, TX UETA, CCOM, eIDAS);
  notices by e-mail to named addresses; in Mexico keep a NOM-151 record for important contracts.

## C-25 Entire agreement and order of documents
- The contract + annexes are the whole deal; if they conflict: SOW, then contract, then
  general terms. Their general terms do not apply unless signed (battle of forms: NL
  BW 6:225 lid 3, first reference wins in B2B).

## C-26 Equity and partnership (co-founders, joint ventures)
- **Default**: letter of intent first (non-binding except confidentiality and costs),
  then a shareholder agreement: split, vesting (4 years, 1-year cliff), good/bad leaver,
  roles and time, decisions needing both, drag/tag, deadlock (mediation then buy-sell),
  IP assigned to the company, cash contributions as convertible loan.
- **Always a lawyer gate.**
